YTL HACKATHON 2026 · ENTERPRISE TRACK A
Project Udyam Digital
Making small businesses go digital in one sitting
Case study and problem statement · Prepared by Ankit Srivastava · October 2026
About this case. Teams submit a working prototype plus a short business case, so we can assess
technical depth and business judgement together. The case uses a fictional operator, Accel
Telecom, and its B2B arm Accel Business. All figures and data are synthetic and built for the
exercise; they are not company data.
Context
Accel Telecom leads India's mobile market and is a top-2 player in home broadband. Its enterprise
arm, Accel Business, serves large corporates well but has a small share of India's 6 crore+ micro,
small and medium enterprises (MSMEs): kirana stores, clinics, coaching centres, small
manufacturers, CA firms and restaurants.
These businesses increasingly need more than a SIM. A typical clinic or retail shop now wants fixed
broadband or a leased line, a few business SIMs, a WhatsApp/SMS channel to reach customers,
cloud email and storage, basic cybersecurity, and CCTV or POS connectivity. Today they buy each
piece from a different vendor, often from a local reseller, with no single bill or support number.
Accel Business already offers most of these products, but sells them one at a time through a field
sales force built for large accounts. The journey for a small business owner is slow and paper-heavy:
 ■ Needs are discovered in a visit by a sales executive, who often pitches whatever is on target that
month
■ A quote takes 3–5 days and comes as a PDF with product codes the owner does not understand
■ KYC needs GST certificate, Udyam registration, address proof and signatory ID, collected
physically or over email
■ Installation and activation take 10–14 days on average, with 3–4 follow-up calls from the customer
■ Each product is billed and supported separately after activation
As a result, cost to acquire a small business is high, conversion is low, and a large share of interested
prospects drop out before activation. Competitors and digital-first players are starting to offer
instant online sign-up for single products.

The problem
Design and build a digital-first acquisition and onboarding journey that lets a small
business owner discover the right bundle, buy it and get it activated with minimal
human effort — on a phone, in their preferred language.
Key questions
1. Which MSME segments should Accel Business prioritise first, and what bundle (products, price
point, contract length) fits each?
2. How should needs discovery work without a salesperson? Think of a conversational advisor (chat
or voice, in Hindi and regional languages) that asks a few questions and recommends a bundle
with a clear monthly price.
3. How can KYC and documentation be made near-instant (e.g. GSTIN/Udyam lookup, document
OCR, DigiLocker, e-sign) while staying compliant with the DPDP Act, 2023 — consent, purpose
limitation and data minimisation?
4. What role should the existing field force and local channel partners play, so the digital journey
adds to them instead of competing with them?
5. How will you measure success? Define the 3–5 metrics that matter (e.g. lead-to-activation
conversion, time to activate, cost of acquisition, 6-month churn) and the targets you would set.
Build challenge
Build a working prototype of the journey covering at least:
Advisor
A GenAI-powered assistant that asks about the business (type, size, locations,
current tools) and recommends a bundle from the product catalogue,
explaining why in plain language
Quote and checkout
A single, transparent monthly price with add-ons the owner can toggle
Smart KYC
Document upload with extraction and validation, mocked lookups of GSTIN and
Udyam data, and a clear consent screen
Tracker
An order status view the owner can check instead of calling support
Stretch goals: a voice or WhatsApp front end; a back-office view for sales or partners showing
leads the advisor could not close; a simple propensity score for which leads to call.
